# Spaced repetition beats marathon cramming

Reviewing the same material at growing intervals — one day, three days,
a week, a month — keeps it available months later. One long session the
night before produces the same *feeling* of knowing and a fraction of
the retention: the material lasts until the exam, not until I actually
need it.

**Evidence:** Cepeda et al. (2006), a synthesis of 254 studies in
Psychological Bulletin — on delayed tests, spaced review beat massed
review in the large majority of comparisons, and the advantage grew the
longer the gap before testing.

**Concrete case:** French vocabulary, winter term. 100 words in one
three-hour evening → about a third still reproducible two weeks later.
The next 100 words in 20 minutes a day across six days — two hours
instead of three → nearly all of them still there at the term test four
weeks on. Same material, same person, less total time; the difference
was the spacing.

**Limit:** on an IMMEDIATE test, massed practice often wins. Cramming is
not irrational for something tomorrow that nothing will ever be built
on. And spacing needs material that can be retrieved as an item — it
schedules retrieval, it does not create comprehension. A chapter I never
understood does not improve by being reviewed six times.

**So what:** anything I will need beyond the next test goes into the
brain in the week I first meet it, with review dates — instead of onto a
pile for one night in June.
